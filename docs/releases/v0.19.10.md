# JBeam Forge 0.19.10: a stock suspension moves its arms, not its shape

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.19.10.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.19.10-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.19.10_amd64.deb** and **JBeam-Forge-0.19.10.AppImage**: Linux.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on. **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game (the app installs it for you).

Projects stay on format v28.

## Why a game suspension was scaled

A reader asked: "is there a reason why by using stock suspension it scales it instead of moving the arms?" There wasn't a good one. Since 0.17 a fitted suspension was stretched sideways as one piece to reach the axle's track: every mesh and every node, subframe and steering rack included, scaled about the centre line. That is not how a modder widens a front end, and it made the set look squashed or pulled.

## What happens now

Set the axle's Track and the set is widened the way you would do it by hand:

- **Hubs, brakes and uprights slide out** by half the difference per side.
- **Arms, links and driveshafts lengthen** from their inner end, so their pivots on the subframe stay where the game put them.
- **A sway bar or beam axle** stretches about the centre line.
- **The subframe, steering rack and inboard brackets stay** as they are.

The viewport shows this as you change the number. The exported jbeam follows the meshes: every node of the set sits where the mesh it rides on now sits, so the beams keep their lengths where nothing moved and the hub nodes land under the wheels. The wheel slots' `nodeOffset` moves out by the same amount, and node positions written as formulas (`$=$trackoffset_F + …`) carry the move too.

## Move any part yourself

Select a single arm, hub, shock or spring of a fitted set and move it like any other mesh. The export now reads where each mesh ended up and moves the nodes that belong to it (a node shared by two moved meshes takes the average). Until now the fitted set's own meshes could be moved in the viewport but the jbeam was exported from the set's original positions, so the car in the game did not match what you saw.

## Under the hood

`suspension/track.ts` holds the per-mesh rule (judged from each mesh's extent across the car); `transplantSuspension` takes the track change and a map of mesh transforms and positions nodes from the flexbody groups they belong to. `ShownPlacement` and the `lateral` stretch are gone from the viewport and the export.
