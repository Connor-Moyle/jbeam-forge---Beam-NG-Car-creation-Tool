# JBeam Forge 0.19.3: springs matched to the car's weight

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.19.3.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.19.3-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.19.3_amd64.deb** and **JBeam-Forge-0.19.3.AppImage**: Linux.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on. **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game (the app installs it for you).

Projects stay on format v28.

## Springs for this car's weight

A suspension borrowed from the game is sprung for the car it came from. Under a car much heavier it sits on its bump stops and rubs the arches (the Covet's under a 1,400 kg body sat on the ground and broke a wing at spawn); under one much lighter it sits high and rides hard. The Suspension workspace now says so on the axle, with the number, and one click, **Springs ×1.4 for this car**, scales every spring rate and damper of the set's strut, spring and shock parts by the weight ratio. Springs ×k keep the ride height the game's car had (deflection is weight over rate); dampers ×k keep the damping ratio. The structural beams of those parts (a strut's tube) are left alone. **As the game made them** puts the numbers back. The scale is an edit on the axle, like the driveline builder's, so it lands in the exported jbeam and in every configuration.

The export's "made for a car about N kg" warning stays for the axles you leave as they are.
