# JBeam Forge 0.19.5: parts held at both ends, latches in the sandbox, lights get their own material in one click

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.19.5.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.19.5-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.19.5_amd64.deb** and **JBeam-Forge-0.19.5.AppImage**: Linux.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on. **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game (the app installs it for you).

Projects stay on format v28.

## Parts held at both ends

A part attaches to its parent's nodes. When the parent reaches only one edge of it (a windscreen on a tub whose nodes end at the cowl, an exocage standing on a body shell, a roof-edge wing), the part was held along that one line and pivoted about it: in the app's sandbox the crawler's windscreen and cage leaned over by nearly a metre, and the game would have shown the same. Now the far end holds on to a rigid relative beside it under the same parent, a roof, a cage, a fixed panel, when the parent can't reach it. Never a door, glass, a light or an add-on, nor anything that moves, so nothing ties a door to the body. Regenerate a part (or the car) to get the new attachments.

## One piece, whatever the model is made of

A part modelled as separate shells (a ladder frame of rails and cross-members, a cage of tubes, a dashboard in pieces) got its beams from each shell's own edges and none between them, so the structure was several islands: the hauler's frame was two halves, front and rear, with nothing joining them, and in the game the truck would have broken in two at spawn. Generation now bridges every island to its nearest neighbour by its closest node pairs, whatever the bracing setting. Regenerate a part to get the bridges; the JBeam workspace's Checks tab already reported "disconnected structures" and is where to look.

## Sunroofs hinge properly

A sunroof's hinge was guessed by the door rule, which hinges up a part's front edge: a flat panel lying down has no height there, so both hinge points landed on one node, a beam of no length went into the jbeam, and the game reported a zero-size beam while the sunroof hung by nothing. A sunroof now hinges along its front edge and latches at the rear, like a bonnet in reverse, and no flat panel of any kind gets both hinge points on one node.

## The sandbox holds doors shut

The settle and stand tests in the Testing workspace had no latch: every hinged door, bonnet and tailgate fell open under gravity and showed as a metre of sag. The sandbox now holds each auto-latching part shut with a beam between its latch pair, as the game's coupler does from spawn, letting go at the latch's strength; the swing and yank tests release it first. The jack stands no longer pick a diffuser's or skirt's featherweight node as a stand, and their damping now scales with the node it holds (a fixed 4000 on a 0.3 kg node was itself unstable and threw three of the showcase cars across the map).

## Lights that share a material

A light whose material is also on other parts can't glow in the game (the export would light the whole body up), so the export warned and left you to make a material by hand in the Materials workspace. The warning now has a button, **Give the lights their own material**: each shared material gets a copy, named after it with "_light", on the light meshes only, and the export's glow map picks it up. One undo step. The practice car, whose whole model shares one material, now exports with no warnings at all.
