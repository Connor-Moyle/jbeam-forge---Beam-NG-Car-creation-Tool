# JBeam Forge 0.19.7: engines you design keep their feet, and go by their own name

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.19.7.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.19.7-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.19.7_amd64.deb** and **JBeam-Forge-0.19.7.AppImage**: Linux.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on. **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game (the app installs it for you).

Projects stay on format v28.

## Designed engines keep their feet

An engine from the Engine workspace takes the weight its design works out, as a scale on the game engine it is built on. A design much lighter than its base (a 2.0 L six on a 3.0 L block came out at 104 kg against the game's 240) made the base engine's nodes so light that its own beams shook them apart at spawn. The scale now stops at what the base engine's beams need to run at the game's 2000 Hz, so the car stands; the designer still shows the design's weight.

## Designed engines go by their own name

In the Configurations panel's engine slot and in the game's parts menu, a designed engine was listed as the game engine it was built on ("Ibishu Hopper 4.1L I6 Engine" for a 2.6 twin-turbo six). Both now use the design's name ("2.6 L inline-6 twin-turbo").

## A body in several versions keeps its engine, suspension and nitrous

A car whose body shell comes in versions (the stock shell and its kit shells as variants of one slot) had its engine, suspensions, plates, hitch and nitrous bottle bolted to whichever body nodes lay nearest, including nodes only one of the shells has. In the game those beams were discarded at spawn ("link target not found") and the engine hung loose; a shell swapped in the parts menu dropped them too. Anything borrowed now bolts only to node names every version of the shell has, and only to the shell (a truck's frame and cab), never to a cage a configuration can leave out. A part's far end no longer holds on to a roll cage either (a seat held by the cage hung on nothing in a version without one). The 2000 Hz check names the node a beam can't find, and the showcase build fails on one. Regenerate to get the new attachments.

## Under the hood

The showcase build (the harness scenario that builds a whole car through the app) now also makes a custom part from the Assign dialog and hinges it along a chosen edge (a glove box lid), designs engines in the Engine workspace on a base engine and builds their 3D models, and writes every configuration slot with its options for planning picks. A sixth showcase car, a Skyline R31 assembled from downloaded files (the body with five kits, seats and wheels from BlenderKit), is built this way: six suspensions, fifteen engines (three designed), twenty-five configurations, every one lint-clean and stable at 2000 Hz. The harness keeps only the newest failed run's data: the kept copies, 3 GB each, had filled the disk.
