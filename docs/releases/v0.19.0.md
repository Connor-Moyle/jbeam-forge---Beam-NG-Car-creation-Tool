# JBeam Forge 0.19.0: trucks with any number of axles, steering range, fuel capacity, three more cars built start to finish

## Downloads

- **JBeam-Forge-Setup-0.19.0.exe**: the installer. It updates any earlier version in place; your settings, projects and library stay.
- **JBeam-Forge-0.19.0-portable.exe**: a single exe, nothing to install.
- **jbeam-forge_0.19.0_amd64.deb** and **JBeam-Forge-0.19.0.AppImage**: Linux.
- **jbeam_forge_blender.py**: the Blender add-on. **jbeam_forge_ingame.zip**: JBeam Forge for inside the game (the app installs it for you).

Projects move to format v28 (an axle's steering range, the car's fuel capacity); older projects open and upgrade as always.

## As many axles as the truck has

**Add axle** in the Suspension workspace puts another axle behind the last; each has its position, a name (it names the slot in the parts menu: Front steer, Second steer, Rear drive…), its track, and **Steers**, which now decides which of the game's suspensions it's offered. Before, a third axle could not be exported: the game's wheel parts state the same wheel nodes on every axle of one end, and the same suspension on two axles named its wheels and differentials twice. Now:

- An axle beyond the first of its end (a twin-steer truck's second front axle, a tandem's second drive axle) **carries its own copies of the wheels and tyres** picked for it in the Wheels picker, renamed with everything else, so the game can build every wheel. The export says so in a warning: that axle's slot offers those copies, not every wheel of the lug count.
- The same game suspension on two axles gets **its own wheel and drivetrain device names** on the second (RL → RL_R2, differential_R → differential_R_R2), wherever its parts name them. The Drive shafts card shows the same names.
- The **Wheels** picker puts matching wheels on every other axle with the same lugs, not just one.

**Steering range** on a steered axle scales its suspension's steering travel (every kind of steering hydro the game's cars use): a twin-steer truck's second axle at 60% turns less than the first. 0% leaves a steered suspension straight.

## Fuel capacity

The **Fuel** card in the Engine workspace sets what the car's tank holds (60 L unless you say); several engines fitted share it between the tanks they ask for. Before, every car carried 60 L.

## Three more cars built through the app, start to finish

`scripts/dev/buildShowcaseCars.mts` now makes five cars, and the desktop harness (`--showcase`, with `--beamng-install`) builds each one the way a user would, now with everything the app can do: the parts' weights, the structure, every axle with the game's suspensions and their options, the wheel picker, engines and fuel, the horn, every opening panel hinged, every needle and pedal animated, scripted wipers, windows, mirrors, seats, sunroof and head unit plus every other script template, a handle on every door and a horn button, interior cameras, plates, hitch, nitrous, a paint design, factory paints, a tuning variable, the configurations, then save, export, lint and the 2000 Hz check of every configuration.

- **Forge Hauler 8x8**: a twin-steer heavy haulage truck with a sleeper cab, two 400 L saddle tanks and a belly tank (1200 L), on four of the MD-Series' axles (the second steer at 60%), with the D-Series desert axles as options; the T-Series 900CUI I6 and 1100CUI V8, the MD-Series diesel and the D-Series diesel V8. Long Haul, Heavy Tow (fifth wheel, ballast, winch bumper) and Expedition (bull bar, roof box, light bar, snorkel, spare) versions.
- **Forge Estate**: a 1990s family wagon with four doors, a tailgate, sunroof, power windows and seats, on the ETK 800's suspension (the wide one and, at the front, the Vivace's rally as options), with four ETK engines. Base, Sport, Rally, Police and Taxi versions.
- **Forge Camper**: a 1980s van conversion with a sliding door, a pop-top, a bed and a kitchen, on the H-Series' axles (4-link and heavy dually as options), with four H-Series engines. Camper, Cargo, Shuttle, Heavy Duty and Race versions.

The harness writes each one out as a project (`.jbforge`) and as a mod; they are not part of this release, and they have not been driven in the game yet.

## refNodes

"The body has no refNodes: regenerate the body" blocked the export of a car whose body nodes came any way but Generate (the JBeam workspace, a car brought in from the game, an older project). The export now places them from the body's nodes itself, and the body's Structure section has a **Place refNodes** button to put them in the project whenever you want (a truck with no body shell uses its frame).

## Fixed

- A suspension whose own wheel part sits in a slot with a node offset (the MD-Series) was read with its wheels 1.3 m in front of the axle and 0.5 m apart, so it was placed a metre off and its track read as 0.97 m. Slot offsets are now baked into node positions when a set is read.
- A truck with a cab and a frame but no body shell left its doors with no parent part, so nothing could be hinged. Doors and the like now hang on the cab.
- The Moving parts panel's animation suggestions read only the renamed mesh names, so a "speedo_needle" shown as "gauge_cluster_2" was never offered. They now read the name the mesh had in the file as well, and the game's suspension and engine meshes are left out (a steering knuckle is not a steering wheel).
- Script templates whose default keys another script already uses get free keys when added (same modifier, next letter or digit); a car with every template had five pairs on one key.
- A node of a borrowed suspension that sits past the plain 2000 Hz solver's limit under its own beams and its bolts (the ETK 800's rear subframe centre, 4 kg under 70 MN/m) is given the weight that puts it inside; the car is a few kilograms heavier.
- A generated node is never lighter than 0.15 kg: a 0.3 kg reverse light's 0.04 kg nodes could not be stepped at 2000 Hz whatever its beams were softened to.
- Parts whose every mesh is set to Ignore no longer warn that they export as an empty slot option; they are left out of the mod.
- The 2000 Hz check reads node weights given as formulas (a part with a weight tuning variable).
- The mod linter accepts "@name" texture maps (the game makes them at run time: a head unit's screen).
- The structure section's weight field and the axle's name can be set by their labels (accessibility).
